# Privacy, Safety and Authorization Model

CyberWolf is an educational resource. Its examples must keep learners, system owners and third parties safe.

## Authorization rules
- Only test a system you own or have explicit written permission to assess.
- Follow the exact target list, activity limits, time window and rules of engagement.
- Stop and report when an activity reaches an out-of-scope asset or causes unexpected impact.
- Use CTF infrastructure and training platforms only as their rules allow.
- Do not test public websites, campus networks, employer systems or third-party assets merely because they are reachable.

## Prohibited content in this repository
Do not contribute instructions or payloads for credential theft, unauthorized access, persistence, stealth, destructive actions, denial of service, exfiltration, malware deployment or evasion of security controls.

## Privacy and publication
- Use synthetic data or data intentionally provided for training.
- Remove usernames, email addresses, public IPs, tokens, cookies, API keys, private keys and personal details from evidence.
- Never commit secrets or real incident artifacts.
- Do not publish another person's work, data or screenshots without permission.
- Use a private local copy for personal learning notes that contain sensitive information.

## Defensive alternatives
Teach detection, secure configuration, threat modeling, safe validation, remediation, incident response and responsible disclosure. Explain why a technique is risky and how to prevent or detect it.
