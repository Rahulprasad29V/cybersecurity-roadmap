# Content Standards

Use these standards for every new learning resource.

## Required learning brief
- **Title:** specific and searchable.
- **Level:** beginner, intermediate or advanced.
- **Prerequisites:** what learners should know first.
- **Estimated effort:** a range, not a guarantee.
- **Learning outcomes:** observable actions using verbs such as explain, compare, configure, analyze, verify or document.
- **Concepts:** concise explanation and authoritative references.
- **Practice:** safe, scoped activity with expected evidence.
- **Knowledge check:** questions that test understanding.
- **Limitations:** what the activity does not establish.
- **Safety:** authorization and privacy boundaries.

## Difficulty guidelines
| Level | Expected work |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Follow a guided process, explain basic concepts, interpret known-good examples |
| Intermediate | Combine concepts, investigate evidence, justify decisions and document trade-offs |
| Advanced | Design a defensible approach, evaluate limitations, compare alternatives and communicate risk |

## Writing rules
- Define acronyms on first use.
- Prefer official vendor, standards-body and project documentation.
- Include publication or review dates for fast-changing guidance.
- Distinguish fact, assumption and recommendation.
- Avoid presenting tool output as proof without validation.
- Do not promise that a checklist guarantees security.
- Never include private target details, credentials or unredacted personal data.

## Link and source policy
Prefer primary sources. Check links during review. For standards, cite the publisher and document version. For products and certification exams, tell readers to verify current pricing, availability, eligibility and exam objectives.
